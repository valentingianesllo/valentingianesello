01 — BACKGROUND

Context

This work was carried out during an academic internship in mathematics during my first year of undergraduate studies (L1), under the supervision of Matthew Cellot, a mathematics professor and PhD student specialising in quantum topology. The internship was devoted to a purely mathematical exploration of graph theory, with a strong emphasis on theoretical mathematics and abstract mathematical structures. The project provided an opportunity to explore connections between graph theory and other areas of mathematics, particularly algebraic topology, while developing an introduction to the topological study of graphs. Graph theory is a fundamental mathematical field with applications extending well beyond pure mathematics, including various areas of theoretical physics.

02 — BOOK OVERVIEW

Content

This book provides a theoretical introduction to graph theory, exploring the mathematical structures and concepts underlying the study of graphs. It develops the subject through a rigorous mathematical approach, introducing fundamental definitions, properties and results while progressively examining more abstract aspects of graph theory. Particular attention is given to the connections between graph theory and topology, including introductory concepts from algebraic topology and the topological structures associated with graphs. The aim is to develop a deeper understanding of graphs from a purely mathematical perspective, while highlighting the connections between different areas of mathematics and the potential relevance of graph theory to theoretical physics.
